why private equity

Numbers that
tell a story.

I'm originally from Nashville, and I came to FSU with my two sisters. I'm a triplet, so growing up was always a little competitive. My first real introduction to finance was in fifth grade, actually, through a school field trip where everyone got assigned a different job for the week. I got finance. I had no idea what that actually meant at the time, but I loved how it felt like a puzzle, how a set of numbers could tell you a whole story about a business.

That interest stuck with me. Going into freshman year, I had my first finance internship at Marriott, working on corporate finance and budgeting. From there, I interned at Movement Mortgage, where I got exposure to loans, interest rates, and how different financing structures actually affect the people borrowing the money.

Most recently, I interned in private equity, where I worked on acquisitions, valuation, and cash flow analysis. That's where things really clicked for me. I loved getting to look at a business from an investor's seat: not just whether the numbers work, but whether this is a company worth owning, and what you'd actually do with it once you did.

Outside of finance, one of the experiences I'm most proud of is founding Giving Gowns in high school: a nonprofit that collects donated formal dresses and gets them to students who might not otherwise be able to afford one. I grew it into a team of around 50 people, and it taught me how much I enjoy building something, solving problems with a team, and seeing work turn into a real outcome for someone else.

I think all of that is what ultimately pulled me toward private equity: the analytical side, the team element, and getting to see a tangible result from the work. What I like most about PE specifically is the horizon: you're not just executing a transaction and moving on, you're taking a real position in a business and living with the outcome, working alongside management, thinking about what actually creates value over years, not just at close. That's the same instinct that made me want to grow Giving Gowns instead of just donating a dress myself. I wanted to build something and stay with it. PE felt like the natural place to put that.

My first job, technically, was running social media for my neighbor's popsicle stand — which sounds like a joke, but it's where I actually learned to think about an audience: what makes someone stop scrolling, and what makes them come back. Years of doing marketing and social for small businesses turned that into a real interest in media and entertainment, and specifically in how engagement actually gets monetized — the difference between a platform with attention and a platform with a business model. I'm a triplet.

One of three, same birthday, nineteen years running — from an incubator to a graduation stage.

Growing up as a triplet means you're competing for everything from day one — the same birthday, the same milestones, the same room, sometimes the same friends — and also that you never actually do anything alone. You learn to push for your own space and to function as a team in the same breath, because the other two are always right there. I think that's a lot of why finance, and PE specifically, made sense to me: it's genuinely competitive, but the actual work only happens on a team, and I'd already spent nineteen years figuring out how to do both at once.
